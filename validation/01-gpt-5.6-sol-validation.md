# GPT-5.6 Sol Independent Validation

**Date:** 11 August 2026  
**Challenger model:** GPT-5.6 Sol (GitHub Copilot subagent)  
**Product principle:** designed 100% by humans.

> **Superseded / historical (Aug 2026).** This report validated the earlier **meetup-first** framing ("Plans That Happen") and is preserved as a record of that decision. The product has since moved to a **fun ephemeral group chat first**, with meetups demoted to a phase-2 wedge — see [concept/04](../concept/04-rooms-and-lanes.md) and the chat-first [14-day experiment](02-14-day-experiment.md). The market facts and copycat/survivorship lessons below still hold; the *recommended atom/loop/north-star* here are the old direction.

## Verdict

> **PIVOT** — **78%** confidence.

Keep:

- the dense campus beachhead;
- private groups and human connection;
- a limited/chronological feed if needed later;
- no vanity metrics or infinite scroll.

Change:

- **core atom:** from *daily Moments* to **Plans**;
- **outcome sold:** not “more authentic posting,” but **hangouts that actually happen**;
- **Moments:** demoted to an optional recap after the meetup.

## Why pivot

1. **Value before network effects.** A single host already benefits from voting on times, RSVPs, and reminders; Moments only become useful once friends join in actively.
2. **Distribution baked into the loop.** Every plan pulls the whole circle into the app, and participants who become hosts bring their own circles — growth rides on the core action, not ads.
3. **A more concrete problem.** “When and where do we meet?” is easier to measure than “wanting to share authentically.”
4. **The daily prompt is already crowded.** BeReal proves this behavior is possible, but it also makes Unggun v1 look like a variation on an already-familiar product.
5. **IRL fits the human-first mission.** The human-made principle is preserved, but positioned as a foundation of trust rather than the main reason people use the product.

## Fact vs inference vs speculation

### Observed facts

- APJII 2024: 221.6 million internet users, 79.5% penetration; Gen Z is the largest group (34.4%); a survey of 8,720 respondents across 38 provinces.
- DataReportal 2026: an estimated 230 million people online and 180 million social identities by the end of 2025; social identities are not necessarily unique people.
- Partiful: around 500 thousand MAU in Q1 2025 (+400% YoY according to Sensor Tower), 2 million new users in early 2025, and >60% of active app users check the app weekly; 90% of app users are in the US.
- BeReal: >40 million active users when acquired by Voodoo for €500 million in June 2024; the company also acknowledged difficulty sustaining growth.
- CHI 2024 research supports BeReal’s spontaneity, but does not prove that every post is automatically authentic or that the mechanic fits Indonesia.

### Reasonably strong inferences

- IRL coordination utility gives a better *cold-start profile* than a new social feed.
- Invite-code circles keep the whole group and its data inside the product, which is better for retention and the experience — at the cost of a higher install bar.
- Campuses are worth testing because of their dense networks, but the best campus is one the founder can actually reach.

### Speculation that must be tested

- Indonesian students will install and adopt a dedicated app instead of just coordinating in their group chat.
- Voting on times increases the number of events that happen.
- A private recap drives a second plan.
- Participants will turn into hosts without founder incentives.
- A human-first approach improves conversion or retention.

## Copycat and survivorship traps

- **BeReal:** don’t conclude “authentic = retentive”; the €500 million acquisition is a strong outcome, but feature expansion and growth challenges show one ritual isn’t enough forever.
- **Poparazzi / Made With Friends:** a viral launch and millions of installs don’t guarantee a sustainable social network.
- **Gas / Fizz:** campuses give fast distribution, but anonymous/compliment mechanics carry safety risks and are easy to copy.
- **Jagat:** 10 million accounts don’t prove DAU, retention, or the safety of location sharing.
- **Partiful:** the evidence for IRL utility is strong, but 90% US users means Indonesian localization is not yet proven.
- **WhatsApp / Discord:** incumbents already have groups and events; Unggun must win by doing one job (turning talk into real meetups) far better, not by becoming another chat app.

## Recommended Unggun v2

**Beachhead:** groups of 4–12 first-year students at **a single campus the founder can reach directly**, who have already tried to plan meals, study sessions, sports, or hangouts in their group chats.

**Atom:** a **Plan** = activity + area/place + 2–3 time options + a list of people.

**Loop:**

1. host creates a Plan in ≤30 seconds;
2. invites the circle into the app (invite code or contacts);
3. members vote on times inside the app;
4. host locks the time/place;
5. participants commit to attend + receive deterministic reminders;
6. a simple check-in;
7. an optional private recap;
8. one of the participants creates the next Plan.

**Remove from MVP activation:** the daily prompt, a generic feed, voice notes, mini-games, streaks, the Campus Board, and campus verification.

**Add:** invite-code circles, in-app voting on times, RSVP/commitment, in-app reminders, an in-app map/place, check-in, a private recap, and a “create the next version” button.

**North-star:** **Plans That Happen** = a plan with ≥3 participants who actually check in. Also measure *repeat plan* at 14/30 days and participant→host conversion.

## Hard decisions

- **Passes the experiment:** build a Plan-first MVP, then add a social recap.
- **Plans happen but don’t repeat:** pivot to a coordination tool for campus organizations/communities.
- **Plans don’t happen:** **KILL the social-app thesis** before spending time building a feed.
